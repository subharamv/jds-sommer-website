Glossary · CAD & BIM terms

CAD & BIM terms, simply explained.

The most important terms from digitalisation, 3D modelling and BIM, from A to Z: from as-built drawings and point clouds to LOD, IFC and CAD conversion.

Glossary terms

    ABCDEFGHILMOPRSTUX

    A

    A0 format
    The largest standard paper size in the ISO 216 series, 841 × 1189 mm. Construction and engineering plans are often A0 or larger, which is why digitisation is often priced per A0 sheet.
    Allplan
    CAD and BIM software from Nemetschek, widely used in architecture and civil engineering in German-speaking countries.
    Archicad
    BIM software from Graphisoft for architectural design, documentation and modelling.
    As-built BIM
    A BIM model of an existing building that reflects its current state, usually created from laser scans or existing plans.
    As-built drawing
    A drawing that shows a building or plant as it was actually built, including all changes made during construction. The basis for operation, maintenance and conversions.

    B

    BIM (Building Information Modelling)
    A working method in which a building is planned, built and operated using a digital 3D model that contains not only geometry but also information such as materials, components and quantities.
    Block
    A named group of CAD objects that is defined once and inserted many times, such as a door, valve or electrical symbol. Changing the definition updates every insertion.

    C

    CAD (Computer-Aided Design)
    Software-based design and drawing. CAD files are exact to scale and fully editable, unlike scans or PDFs.
    CAD conversion
    Turning paper or PDF drawings into editable CAD drawings: lines, arcs, text and symbols become CAD objects that can be edited and reused.
    CAD restructuring
    Bringing existing CAD files into line with a uniform standard: layers, line types, text styles, blocks and drawing frames.
    CAD standard
    A set of rules for how drawings are structured: layer names, colours, line types, text styles, blocks and title blocks. Many companies and public clients have their own.
    CAD-to-BIM
    Converting existing 2D CAD drawings into an intelligent 3D BIM model with walls, slabs, doors and other components.
    CAFM (Computer-Aided Facility Management)
    Software for managing buildings, rooms, areas and technical equipment over their whole life cycle. CAFM systems need structured CAD plans with clean room and area polygons, which we prepare to the requirements of your system.

    D

    DGN
    The native file format of Bentley MicroStation, widely used in infrastructure, energy and plant engineering.
    Dimensioning
    Adding measurements to a drawing: dimension lines, values and tolerances that show the size and position of elements.
    DPI (dots per inch)
    The resolution of a scan. The higher the dpi, the finer the detail: thin lines and small text in plans need a higher resolution than plain text documents.
    DWG
    The native file format of Autodesk AutoCAD and the most widely used format for 2D and 3D CAD drawings.
    DXF
    A CAD exchange format developed by Autodesk that most CAD programs can read and write.

    E

    E57
    An open, vendor-neutral file format for 3D point clouds and the images taken with them (ASTM E2807), used to exchange laser scan data between programs.

    F

    Facility management (FM)
    The operation and management of buildings and their technical systems. Up-to-date, editable plans are an essential basis for it.

    G

    Georeferencing
    Assigning real-world coordinates to a scanned map or drawing, so it lines up with other data in a GIS or CAD coordinate system.
    GIS (Geographic Information System)
    A system for capturing, managing and analysing location-based data, for example utility networks, land registers or city maps.

    H

    Hatching
    A pattern of lines or symbols that fills an area in a drawing, for example to show a material such as concrete, masonry or insulation in a section.
    HVAC
    Heating, ventilation and air conditioning: the building services that control temperature and air quality.

    I

    IFC (Industry Foundation Classes)
    An open, manufacturer-independent BIM exchange format (ISO 16739) that lets different BIM programs share models.

    L

    Large-format scanning
    Scanning plans and drawings larger than A3, up to A0 and beyond, on wide-format scanners built for plans and maps.
    Laser scanning (3D)
    Measuring buildings, plants or objects with a laser scanner that captures their surfaces as millions of measured points. The result is a point cloud.
    Layer
    A level in a CAD drawing used to group elements such as walls, dimensions or text, so they can be shown, hidden or edited together.
    Line type
    The pattern of a line in a CAD drawing, such as continuous, dashed or dash-dot. Line types carry meaning, for example hidden edges or centre lines.
    LOD (Level of Development)
    Describes how detailed and reliable a BIM model is, from LOD 100 (rough massing) to LOD 500 (verified as-built state).

    M

    MEP
    Mechanical, electrical and plumbing: the technical building services modelled alongside architecture and structure.
    MicroStation
    CAD software from Bentley Systems, common in infrastructure, utilities and plant engineering. Its native format is DGN.
    Model space and layout
    The two areas of a CAD file: the model is drawn at full size in model space, and layouts (paper space) place it at a scale on a sheet with a title block for printing.

    O

    OCR (Optical Character Recognition)
    Automatic recognition of text in scanned documents and drawings, so that it becomes searchable and editable.

    P

    PDF to CAD
    Converting PDF drawings into editable CAD files. Vector PDFs often still contain the lines; scanned PDFs contain only pixels and need to be converted into CAD geometry.
    Point cloud
    The result of a 3D laser scan: millions of measured points that capture the geometry of a building, plant or object precisely.
    Point cloud to 2D CAD (Scan-to-CAD)
    Creating precise 2D floor plans, sections and elevations from 3D laser scan data (point clouds). The result is a to-scale, layered CAD drawing of the current as-built state, without modelling the whole building in 3D.
    Polyline
    A single CAD object made of connected line and arc segments, often used for outlines, walls and room boundaries so that areas can be calculated.

    R

    Raster data
    Images made of pixels, such as scans of paper plans. They can be viewed, but not edited like CAD lines.
    Registration
    Joining the individual scans of a laser scan survey into one point cloud in a common coordinate system.
    Reverse engineering
    Creating a 3D CAD model and drawings from an existing physical part, for example to manufacture a spare part without original drawings.
    Revision
    A numbered change to a drawing. Each revision is recorded in the title block or a revision table, so everyone works from the current state.
    Revit (RVT)
    BIM software from Autodesk for architecture, structure and building services. RVT is its native file format.

    S

    Scale
    The ratio between a drawing and reality, such as 1:100 or 1:50. CAD models are drawn at full size; the scale only applies when the drawing is printed.
    Scan-to-BIM
    Creating a BIM model of an existing building from laser scan data (point clouds).
    Scan-to-CAD
    Another name for point cloud to 2D CAD: creating to-scale 2D CAD drawings from laser scan data.
    Shapefile (SHP)
    A widely used GIS vector format from Esri that stores points, lines and areas together with their attributes.
    STEP
    A standardised exchange format (ISO 10303) for 3D CAD models, common in mechanical engineering and manufacturing.
    STL
    A 3D format that describes surfaces as a mesh of triangles, common in 3D printing and as an output of 3D scanning.

    T

    TIFF
    A raster image format often used for scanned plans, because it stores images without lossy compression.
    Title block
    The box on a drawing sheet with the project details: drawing title and number, scale, date, revision and the people responsible.

    U

    Utility map
    A map of underground or overhead networks such as water, gas, electricity and telecoms. Many exist only on paper and are digitised into a GIS.

    X

    Xref (external reference)
    A drawing linked into another drawing instead of copied into it, for example a floor plan used as the base for building services. Changes to the source show up wherever it is referenced.

    FAQ

    Frequently asked questions about working together.

    Short answers to the questions behind the terms.

    Still have a question? We are happy to advise you personally on +49 (0)69 / 905 469-0 or by email at info@jds-sommer.de.

    What is drawing-to-CAD conversion?

    We convert paper drawings, PDFs and scanned plans into precise, editable 2D CAD files, keeping the geometry, dimensions, text, symbols and layer structure you need. You receive AutoCAD DWG, DXF, MicroStation DGN or, where agreed, Allplan files.

    What is Scan-to-BIM?

    We turn registered point clouds and laser-scan data into detailed 3D BIM models of the captured building or facility, based on the agreed modelling requirements. You receive the model in Revit (RVT), IFC, Allplan or Archicad.

    What is as-built BIM modelling?

    A BIM model of a building or facility as it exists today, created from point clouds, laser scans, existing drawings and survey information. It is the basis for later planning and documentation.

    Which level of detail (LOD) can you model?

    Anything from LOD 100 to LOD 500. You define the level of detail and with it the accuracy of your model, including components such as walls, ceilings, stairs, windows and doors with their properties.

    How do you convert paper and PDF drawings into CAD?

    CAD specialists convert your paper or PDF drawings into editable CAD drawings on screen, and engineers check every drawing before delivery. Drawing structure and technical information are preserved according to the agreed scope.

    What are as-built drawings?

    Drawings that document the actual state of a building, including all changes made during construction and later conversions. We create them from construction and permit drawings, marked-up plans, point clouds or site measurements, as floor plans, sections and elevations to your CAD standard.
