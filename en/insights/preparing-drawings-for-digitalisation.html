5 Steps to Prepare Your Drawings for Digitalisation

Good preparation saves time and money. Here's a practical checklist for getting your paper plans, microfilm, or scans ready before sending them for CAD conversion.

4 min read
JDS Sommer Team

Share

Whether you're sending a single drawing or thousands of archive plans, a few minutes of preparation can significantly reduce turnaround time and costs. Here are the five steps we recommend before sending your drawings for CAD digitalisation.

1 Check Drawing Condition

Before scanning or sending paper originals, take a moment to assess their condition:

  • Legibility: Can all text, dimensions, and line work be read clearly?
  • Completeness: Are there missing sections, tears, or water damage?
  • Folds and creases: Deep folds can create shadows that obscure content during scanning

Pro tip

If a drawing is badly damaged, let us know upfront. We can often work with partial data and fill in gaps from context, but it's important to set expectations early.

2 Verify Scale Information

Accurate scaling is essential for usable CAD output. Make sure each drawing includes:

  • A stated scale (e.g., 1:100, 1:50) in the title block
  • A scale bar or at least one known dimension we can use to verify
  • The original paper size (A0, A1, etc.) if not obvious

If the original scale is unknown, provide a reference measurement. Even a door width or column spacing helps us calibrate the output correctly.

3 Scan at the Right Resolution

If you're scanning the drawings yourself rather than sending paper originals, scan quality matters:

  • Minimum 300 DPI for line drawings
  • 400 DPI for drawings with fine detail or small text
  • Black and white or greyscale is usually sufficient (colour adds file size without benefit for most technical drawings)
  • TIFF or PDF format preserves quality better than JPEG

Common mistake

Scanning at low resolution (150 DPI or less) to save disk space. The time spent manually interpreting unclear content costs more than the storage saved.

4 Organise and Name Files

Clear organisation speeds up the quoting process and reduces errors:

  • Use consistent file naming that identifies the drawing (e.g., Building-Floor-DrawingType-Number)
  • Group files into folders by building, floor, or discipline
  • Include a simple spreadsheet listing each file with its description if you have many drawings

5 Define Your Requirements

The more clearly you specify what you need, the faster we can deliver exactly that:

  • Output format: DWG, DXF, DGN, or another?
  • CAD version: AutoCAD 2018, 2024, etc.
  • Layer structure: Do you have a standard we should follow?
  • What to include: All content, or specific elements only?
  • Turnaround time: Standard delivery (3 to 5 working days), or an express request by arrangement?

Quick Checklist

  • Drawings are legible and complete
  • Scale information or reference dimension included
  • Scans at 300+ DPI in TIFF or PDF format
  • Files named and organised logically
  • Output requirements specified
